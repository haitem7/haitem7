Why Sigoise

Sigoise is the olive of western Algeria. It grows on the limestone hillsides between Tlemcen and Ghazaouet, where summers are dry and nights are cool. That day-to-night swing concentrates polyphenols instead of diluting them.

Its flesh-to-stone ratio yields 18 to 22% under cold extraction. On the palate: raw artichoke, green almond, a clean bitterness and a pepper that stays in the throat. It holds up to heat and it does not disappear into a vinaigrette.

From tree to tank

Six operations, in order. Each one carries a time or temperature constraint, and that is where the quality of a lot is decided.

  1. 1

    Picking

    Pneumatic combs and ground nets. Fruit travels in ventilated 22 kg crates, never in sacks — a sack crushes the olive and raises acidity before it reaches the mill.

    ≤ 6 h to the mill

  2. 2

    Intake and washing

    Weighing, de-leafing, cold-water washing. Every delivery is assigned a lot number that follows the oil all the way to the container.

    Lot opened

  3. 3

    Milling

    Stainless hammer mill, continuous flow. The paste is formed with as little air contact as the line allows.

    Stainless steel

  4. 4

    Malaxation

    Forty minutes under nitrogen, 26 °C maximum. Run it warmer and you gain yield and lose aroma; we decide for aroma.

    26 °C max

  5. 5

    Extraction

    Two-phase centrifugal decanter. No added water, so no polyphenols washed out and no olive mill wastewater to treat.

    Two-phase

  6. 6

    Storage

    Nitrogen-blanketed 12,000 l stainless tanks at 16 °C, in the dark. Packing happens against an order, never in advance.

    16 °C, under nitrogen
